# Exemplar-paper routing across HCI-stack

Use complete exemplar papers as worked examples of research structure, methodological reasoning,
visual explanation, and communication. Do not imitate sentences or treat prestige as evidence that
every choice is correct.

For every exemplar, record:

| Field | Required analysis |
|---|---|
| Identity | Exact version, citation, venue, year, and full-copy location/source ID |
| Section | Exact section, page, figure, table, or appendix examined |
| Structural move | What job the section performs and in what sequence |
| Evidentiary move | How claims are supported and bounded |
| Strength | What makes the move effective |
| Limitation | What is project-specific or should not transfer |
| Adaptation | How the current project might apply the principle without copying |
| Phase decision | Which current choice or plan this example informs |

Keep exemplar analysis separate from related-work evidence. An exemplar belongs in the related-work
matrix only when its research is substantively relevant to the project's problem or approach.

## Phase routing

| Phase | Read these exemplar sections | Learn and transfer |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Office Hours | Abstract, Introduction, conclusion, contribution summary | What kinds of problems become consequential HCI contributions; how scope and feasibility align |
| 1 — Motivation and contributions | Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, contribution statements, Discussion opening | Motivation evidence chains, why-now, positive prior-work synthesis, gap precision, empirical waist, contribution-layer ranking, broader-HCI reopening |
| 2 — Solve the problem well | Formative work, design process, system/implementation, iterations, pilots, technical/performance evaluation | How user evidence changes design; alternatives and rationales; reproducible system description; meaningful performance thresholds and pilot-driven revision |
| 3 — Validation | Research questions, study design, measures, baselines, analysis, Results, limitations, field deployment | Methodological precedents, construct operationalization, mixed-method integration, uncertainty, evidence-to-claim calibration, transfer boundaries |
| 4 — Visual presentation | Hero figure, system diagrams, study diagrams, charts, tables, captions | Visual argument, reading order, encodings, comparison clarity, captions that state takeaway and boundary |
| 5 — Paper writing | Corresponding section of each exemplar plus whole-paper argument | Section architecture, transitions, hourglass movement, lexical consistency, evidence placement, tone, contribution through-line |
| 6 — Video | Paper narrative, hero scenario, figures, and any available official preview/video | One consequential problem, one memorable mechanism, visible before/after experience, scoped evidence, broader relevance in 30–90 seconds |

Every phase accepts its own exemplar set and may reuse prior exemplar analyses, but prior outputs are
optional. If no exemplar is supplied, propose three to five candidates and let the author choose
before treating any as a house style.

## Current four-paper pattern library

When the author supplies the complete works, use these as initial exemplars:

- **StrengthGaming:** authoritative public-health and exercise-science evidence becomes a concrete
  design constraint; useful in Phases 1, 2, and 5.
- **MRDrum:** broad activity class narrows to a specific empirical waist and reopens through a
  shared temporal-motor mechanism; useful in Phases 1, 4, 5, and 6.
- **TurnAhead:** a dated enabling change and mechanism-level closest-predecessor comparison sharpen
  why-now and novelty; useful in Phases 1, 2, and 5.
- **AirRacket:** a diagnostic limitation reveals a perceptual insight and reusable design strategy;
  useful in Phases 1, 2, 3, and 5.

These are starting hypotheses about their strengths. Re-open the complete papers and examine the
phase-relevant sections before transferring a pattern.
