# Motivation-claim strengthening loop

Resolve each consequential motivation or problem-definition claim into its strongest defensible
state — supported, bounded, contradicted, superseded, retired, or assigned to the smallest original
project study that could establish it — so `hypothesis` and `unsupported` never become permanent
labels that reports merely repeat.

## Scope

Run the loop over every active claim-ledger row marked `hypothesis` or `unsupported`, first on
claims fixing whether the target behavior exists and for whom, its prevalence and consequences,
current practice and where it fails, why the problem is timely, the unmet need, and decisive
terminology. Also inventory mechanism, experience, and outcome hypotheses about the proposed
system, but
do not pretend external adjacency validates a system that has not been built or studied. Skip no row
silently: rejected, superseded, and duplicate claims stay visible with their disposition.

## Five resolution routes

Assign exactly one primary route before searching:

| Route | Use when | Resolution evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `A — external research` | published evidence can test the phenomenon, magnitude, or construct | full primary studies, syntheses, contradictions |
| `B — official practice audit` | the claim concerns current products, policies, or documented controls | dated first-party documentation plus version/scope checks |
| `C — author/project evidence` | the claim concerns the team's users, workflow, or unmet need | a checked artifact or bounded formative evidence need |
| `D — future system evaluation` | the claim concerns the intervention's mechanism, experience, adoption, or outcome | a Phase 2 premise test or Phase 3 evaluation |
| `E — retire or supersede` | the claim is vague, duplicated, contradicted, loaded, or unsafe | explicit replacement or rejection rationale |

A route is not an evidence state: route `D` leaves the claim a hypothesis until the project
generates the required evidence.

## Resolution cycle

Read [research-discovery-recall.md](research-discovery-recall.md) and create
`motivation-claim-research-queue.md` from the asset template, then:

1. **Enumerate and normalize.** Copy every active `hypothesis` and `unsupported` ledger row into the
   queue, add motivation-chain gaps absent from the ledger, and rewrite each item as one narrow,
   falsifiable claim, splitting prevalence, association, causality, mechanism, current capability,
   experience, and comparative effectiveness rather than joining them.
2. **Triage.** Assign one primary route with a reason, marking rejected or superseded claims first,
   and resolve target behavior, current practice, unmet need, consequence, and why-now before
   proposed-system mechanisms.
3. **Discover and challenge recall.** Run the layered, replayable search of
   [research-discovery-recall.md](research-discovery-recall.md), including its claim-facet matrix,
   independent retrieval challenger, contradiction search, and non-title/non-author controls.
4. **Acquire and audit.** Open the full source, tag it under
   [evidence-protocol.md](evidence-protocol.md), and record the decisive locator, uncertainty,
   limitation, and re-review trigger.
5. **Resolve and repeat.** Use one of `supported-bounded`, `narrowed`, `contradicted`, `superseded`,
   `retired`, `routed-to-project-evidence`, `needs-author-source-access`, or `still-open`, updating
   the claim ledger and motivation evidence map so the old broad wording no longer governs. Promote
   every title, DOI, or canonical URL found inside notes, references, reports, and candidates into
   its own terminal row, and re-sweep the ledger after every research batch, because new findings
   often split or invalidate other claims.

## Research stopping rule

The gate reaches `MOTIVATION_CLAIM_AUDIT_COMPLETE` only when every active claim marked `hypothesis`
or `unsupported` has a precise claim and explicit route; a documented full-source or
official-practice search including contradictory evidence when externally resolvable; the recall
evidence required by [research-discovery-recall.md](research-discovery-recall.md) for every
high-impact external route; a disposition and bounded replacement wording; and either sufficient
evidence for its current use or the smallest named project evidence action. Proving the proposed
system's mechanism is not required in Phase 1; such claims stay hypotheses only when explicitly
routed to later evaluation with an operational construct, comparator, and evidence need.

Listing open claims in a report does not complete the gate: while a high-impact external or
official-practice route remains researchable, keep searching. If a decision-relevant full source is
blocked, follow the author-access protocol and ask for the smallest access action; do not ask the
author to judge or choose the claim.

## Claim-type safeguards

- **Prevalence:** require a target population, sampling frame, episode definition, geography, and
  timeframe; adjacent daily screen-time prevalence is not target-behavior prevalence.
- **Current practice and failure:** distinguish reminders, soft limits, hard blocks, schedules,
  allowlists, notification suppression, and override behavior, and likewise among ignoring a prompt,
  bypassing a control, disabling a feature, uninstalling, attrition, and abandonment; never compress
  a mixed control landscape into “binary.”
- **Why now and terminology:** require a dated change plus evidence that it affects the target
  problem, and trace a term's origin, operational definition, and validated measures; a product
  release establishes capability, not importance, and a vivid popular phrase may stay
  participant-facing without becoming the academic construct.
- **Proposed-system mechanism/outcome:** external evidence may justify plausibility or a design
  probe, but only direct project evidence establishes that the system produces the claimed outcome.
