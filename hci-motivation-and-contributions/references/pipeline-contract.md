# Phase 1 pipeline contract

## Independent entry

Every HCI-stack phase must be useful without artifacts from an earlier phase, so reconstruct the
minimum starting state from whatever exists and never require the user to run, repeat, or approve a
prior skill. Import existing artifacts without overwriting the originals, preserve their provenance
and status, treat their conclusions as hypotheses until this phase verifies them at its required
depth, and ask only about consequential gaps they leave open. Missing pipeline artifacts are not
research flaws.

When a citation catalog exists, preserve each exact work's stable `citation_key` across handoffs.
Later phases merge `references.csv` rather than rebuilding identities from author shorthand.

## Overlap is deliberate

Office Hours screens and compares research ideas and remains valuable by itself: it investigates
motivation, related work, positioning, and contribution far enough for an actionable research brief.
Phase 1 revisits those topics at a different resolution:

| Concern | Office Hours | Phase 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Decide which idea deserves attention | Develop and validate a selected research direction |
| Literature | Representative landscape | Full-copy corpus, recursive citation chaining, forward citations |
| Motivation | Credible evidence and major gaps | Claim-level evidence map with method, strength, locator, and why-now |
| Related work | Identify major alternatives | Closest-work comparison, inheritance, differences, and saturation log |
| Positioning | Useful chart and significance judgment | Multiple chart views grounded in a stable verified corpus |
| Contributions | Initial hypothesis | Alternative scoped packages tied to Phase 2/3 evidence requirements |
| Output | Standalone research brief | Research framing outline and optional Phase 2 handoff |

Do not weaken Office Hours to eliminate overlap. Do not merely repeat its conclusions in Phase 1.

## Feedback loops

The pipeline is not linear: related-work discovery, formative research, prototyping, pilots,
summative or field studies, visual explanation, and writing can each invalidate the problem, gap,
approach, contribution, stakeholder, system, or task. Each handoff must name explicit return
conditions rather than treating earlier decisions as irreversible.

## Boundaries with later phases

- **Phase 2 — solve the problem well:** formative research, UX/system design, implementation,
  prototype feedback, pilots, system-performance evaluation. Outlines and plans.
- **Phase 3 — validate:** summative and field studies, prior-method grounding, analysis plans,
  validation logic. Outlines and plans.
- **Phase 4 — visual presentation:** hero figure, diagrams, charts, tables, captions.
- **Phase 5 — paper writing:** manuscript architecture and prose, Abstract through limitations.
- **Phase 6 — video:** a 30–90 second CHI submission story grounded in the completed project.

Phase 1 may identify requirements and questions for later phases, but must not pretend to complete
their work.
