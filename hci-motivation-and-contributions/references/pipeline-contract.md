# Phase 1 pipeline contract

## Independent entry

Every HCI-stack phase must be useful without artifacts from an earlier phase. Ask what materials
exist, inspect them, and reconstruct the minimum starting state. Never require the user to run,
repeat, or approve a prior skill.

When earlier artifacts exist:

- import them without overwriting the originals;
- preserve their provenance and status;
- treat conclusions as hypotheses until this phase verifies them at its required depth; and
- ask only about consequential gaps not already answered by the materials.

When they do not exist, proceed from the user's idea, notes, papers, observations, prototype, study
materials, or partial results. Missing pipeline artifacts are not research flaws.

When a citation catalog exists, preserve each exact work's stable `citation_key` across handoffs.
Later phases merge `references.csv` rather than rebuilding identities from author shorthand.

## Overlap is deliberate

Office Hours screens and compares research ideas, but remains valuable by itself: it can investigate
motivation, related work, positioning, and contribution enough to give the user an actionable
research brief.

Phase 1 revisits these topics at a different resolution:

| Concern | Office Hours | Phase 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Decide which idea deserves attention | Develop and validate a selected research direction |
| Literature | Representative landscape | Full-copy corpus, recursive citation chaining, forward citations |
| Motivation | Credible evidence and major gaps | Claim-level evidence map with method, strength, locator, and why-now |
| Related work | Identify major alternatives | Closest-work comparison, inheritance, differences, and saturation log |
| Positioning | Useful chart and significance judgment | Multiple chart views grounded in a stable verified corpus |
| Contributions | Initial hypothesis | Alternative scoped packages tied to Phase 2/3 evidence requirements |
| Output | Standalone research brief | Research framing outline and optional Phase 2 handoff |

Do not weaken Office Hours to eliminate overlap. Do not merely repeat its conclusions in Phase 1.

## Feedback loops

The pipeline is not linear. Later evidence may trigger a return to an earlier phase:

- related-work discovery may invalidate the problem, gap, approach, or contribution;
- formative research may reveal a different need or stakeholder;
- design and prototyping may expose adoption, feasibility, safety, or interaction costs;
- pilot or performance work may require a different system or task;
- summative or field studies may narrow claims or reveal a more important phenomenon;
- visual explanation may expose a confused comparison or contribution;
- writing or video work may reveal that the project lacks a coherent argument.

Each handoff must name explicit return conditions rather than treating earlier decisions as
irreversible.

## Boundaries with later phases

- **Phase 2 — solve the problem well:** formative research, UX/system design, implementation,
  prototype feedback, pilots, and system-performance evaluation. Output outlines and plans.
- **Phase 3 — validate:** summative and field studies, prior-method grounding, quantitative and
  qualitative analysis plans, and validation logic. Output outlines and plans.
- **Phase 4 — visual presentation:** hero figure, diagrams, charts, tables, and captions based on
  decisions and data.
- **Phase 5 — paper writing:** manuscript architecture and prose, including Abstract,
  Introduction, Related Work, method, results, discussion, and limitations.
- **Phase 6 — video:** a 30–90 second CHI submission story grounded in the completed project.

Phase 1 may identify requirements and questions for later phases, but must not pretend to complete
their work.
